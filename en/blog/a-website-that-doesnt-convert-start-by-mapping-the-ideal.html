A website that doesn’t convert? Start by mapping the ideal customer journey

October 4, 2026 • 2 min read

A website that doesn’t convert often fails to answer one simple question: what is the primary goal of your visitors? Before choosing a design or features, start by clarifying the journey you want them to take. An effective website should guide users toward a clear action, without friction.

Why map the customer journey?

A well-defined customer journey helps avoid common pitfalls: - A menu that’s too broad, burying key information. - Pages that don’t meet visitor expectations. - Navigation that leads nowhere.

By starting with the end goal (a purchase, a booking, a contact), you can build a logical path, step by step. Every page, link, and button should serve this journey.

How to map the ideal customer journey

1. Define the main goal What do you want visitors to do when they arrive on your site? - Book an appointment? - Buy a product? - Get in touch?

2. Identify key steps Break the journey into simple stages: - Homepage → Overview → Offer → Call-to-action. - Or: Homepage → Catalog → Product page → Cart → Checkout.

3. Remove friction - Cut unnecessary steps. - Simplify forms. - Make information accessible in one click.

4. Test and adjust Have your journey tested by customers or close contacts. Their feedback will help spot bottlenecks.

Concrete example

Imagine a hair salon: - Goal : Enable online booking. - Ideal journey : Homepage → Gallery → Services → Availability → Booking. - Outcome : Less time wasted on emails, more satisfied customers.

Your next step

An effective website isn’t built by chance. It’s designed around your visitors’ needs and your business goals. If you want a website that converts, start by mapping the ideal customer journey.
