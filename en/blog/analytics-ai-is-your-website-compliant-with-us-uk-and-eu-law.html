Analytics AI: is your website compliant with US, UK and EU law?

October 28, 2026 • 2 min read

One website, several legal systems

Selling online often means selling across borders. Yet every zone has its own rules on cookies, personal data, legal notices and the right of withdrawal. A website that is fully compliant in Belgium can still be a problem for an American or British customer. Analytics AI, the compliance analysis tool from JCODE Agency, now covers three zones: the United States, the United Kingdom and the European Union.

The principle is unchanged: you paste your website address, choose the legislation to check, and the tool reads your public pages, checks the applicable requirements and ranks the fixes by priority. No code to copy, nothing to install.

What each zone covers

European Union. The analysis relies in particular on the GDPR, the ePrivacy Directive for cookie consent and consumer protection rules: pre-contractual information, the seller's legal notices and the 14-day right of withdrawal. For the EU, the member states offered depend on your location, detected from your IP address, so the list can differ from one visitor to another.

United Kingdom. The UK GDPR and the Data Protection Act 2018 govern personal data, while the PECR rules apply to cookies and marketing. For consumer sales, the Consumer Rights Act 2015 and the Consumer Contracts Regulations 2013 set the information and cancellation requirements.

United States. The law is fragmented: state privacy laws such as California's, federal rules on advertising and commercial practices, and accessibility requirements. That is why a check per zone is useful: what is enough elsewhere is not always enough here.

What a non-compliant website risks

Penalties vary by zone, and Analytics AI shows them next to each check. In the European Union, the GDPR provides for fines of up to 20 million euros or 4 percent of annual worldwide turnover, and each member state adds its own penalties. In the United Kingdom, the UK GDPR provides for fines of up to 17.5 million pounds or 4 percent of annual worldwide turnover. In the United States there is no single figure: penalties depend on the state and the competent authority, and are often calculated per violation.

These amounts are ceilings, not certainties. They mainly show why it is better to spot the gaps before an authority or a customer does.

How to use it

Go to the Analytics AI page, choose your legislation, paste your website address and start the analysis. You get an overall score, the detail of each check with the relevant law, performance and security indicators, and AI recommendations ranked by priority. The audit is free.

If you would rather not fix things yourself, the turnkey compliance fix at 400 euros handles the gaps for one site and one language: legal pages, cookies and right of withdrawal, with a new audit before and after.

Analytics AI is an indicative automatic analysis of public pages. It is not legal advice and no result guarantees the absence of a penalty.
