Why automation alone isn’t enough (and how to avoid the pitfalls)

Many businesses fall into the trap of premature automation. They pick a tool, set up a basic automation, and end up with a process that’s even stiffer than before—but this time, it runs faster… and is harder to fix.

The classic mistake? Automating a broken process. Imagine a tradesman who automates his booking system without first clarifying his available time slots or standardizing his client responses. The result: customers receive confirmations for already booked slots, and the automation worsens the problem instead of solving it.

At JCODE, we start with a simple truth: automation isn’t decreed, it’s earned. Before configuring a single workflow, you must audit, optimize, and only then automate. That’s the foundation of our A.O.A. method.

The A.O.A. method: a 3-step approach to automate safely

Our method consists of three key phases, which represent 80% of the real work behind automation. Tools like Make.com, Claude skills, etc., only come into play at the end.

### 1️⃣ Audit: map your real processes (and uncover invisible dysfunctions)

Instead of starting from a theoretical summary, we walk through the actual process with you, step by step, on screen. Every action is questioned:

  • Why does this step exist?
  • Who triggers it?
  • What happens if we remove it?
  • How do we know it’s done?

This phase often reveals surprises:

  • Empty lists in your tools (steps declared but never executed).
  • Duplicated data (the same information entered in two different tools = two versions of the truth).
  • Excess tools (each tool adds an input, an export, and a risk of error).

Deliverable: An audit document that becomes the single source of truth for the rest of the project. This isn’t just a report—it’s a precise map of your current processes, with identified quick wins to optimize before automating.

### 2️⃣ Optimize: remove the fluff and standardize

Once the audit is complete, we move to optimization. The goal? Reduce complexity and clarify responsibilities.

  • Remove unnecessary steps: Detours, copy-paste, back-and-forth between tools.
  • One source of truth per piece of information: Zero duplication. If a data point is used in multiple tools, it should only be entered once.
  • Choose the right tool: Reduce your tool stack to the essentials. For example, if you use Google Sheets, Airtable, and Notion for booking management, we simplify to a single central tool.

Result: A shorter, clearer, and more reliable process, ready to be automated.

### 3️⃣ Automate: build reliable, iterable skills

Only after auditing and optimizing do we move to automation. At JCODE, we call these automations skills: `skill.md` files that describe one well-executed task, complete with concrete examples and instructions for iteration.

Our golden rules for reliable skills:

  • One skill = one task: For example, a skill to send a reminder email to overdue clients, another to generate a PDF invoice, etc.
  • Human approval before automatic sending is activated: Even for a skill that responds to prospects, automatic sending is only enabled after validation on real volume. Trust must be proven, not assumed.
  • Build → Test → Iterate → Lock: Each skill is tested on a real case before being generalized. Version 2 is usually the right one.

Concrete example: A hospitality client had 24 manual steps for managing bookings and sending client reminders. After A.O.A., we reduced this to 5 skills: - 22 automated steps (bookings automatically confirmed, personalized reminders, etc.). - 2 steps kept intentionally manual (final validation by the manager, handling exceptional cancellations).
